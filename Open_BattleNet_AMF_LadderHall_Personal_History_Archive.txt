This is the longer version of my story. The README tells it in a few
chapters; this file keeps the details: how the dueling ladders worked,
what we actually tested, who taught me things, and what survived from
those years.

I was HU_Thor before I became Azreal]AMF[. I'm writing about roughly
2003 to 2008, from memory and from old characters, emails, screenshots,
clan writing, and public technical guides. A surviving screenshot can
show what happened on screen, but not every condition of a ranked match.
A clan record can tell us what AMF claimed at the time without being
an independent ladder export. I won't pretend those are the same thing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
01. WHAT THIS WORLD WAS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Diablo II Open Battle.net was unlike Closed Battle.net because the
character file lived on your own machine. Hero Editor made it possible
to edit equipment, charms, sockets, skills, auras, and values far past
anything a normal character could obtain. Then you could bring that
edited character into a multiplayer game against other edited builds.

This sounds like a competition to enter the largest number. It wasn't.
Damage could overflow or be reduced unexpectedly. One aura could make
another build stronger rather than weaker. The exact value of mana
could matter more than having millions of it. Even who joined a game
first could change the result.

What happened outside matches mattered just as much. We experimented,
tested single values, compared outcomes, and tried to infer how the game
calculated things. A duel against a strong player might be a real
challenge, or it might be a test to learn how their defenses worked.
A failed attack could reveal a vulnerability. An alternate identity
could help conceal what you were investigating.

LadderHall gave those discoveries somewhere to matter. The ladders,
referees, forums, clan challenges, screenshots, and reputations made
a small experimental community feel like a competitive world. One
new counter could invalidate what everyone thought was unbeatable.

To me, the main competition wasn't execution. It was understanding
the game better than whoever was standing across from you.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
02. EVIDENCE STANDARDS AND SOURCE INVENTORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is a personal history, not a reconstructed official tournament
database. I have tried to keep those two things separate.

There are several kinds of material behind it.

The most direct surviving records include an AMF clan history from
December 2006, old emails with attachments, character saves and
character exports, archived forum pages, and actual duel screenshots.
The AMF document records a seven-of-seven 1.11 ladder #1 claim under
my Azreal]AMF[ name. That tells us the clan was making the claim in
2006. We do not have seven separately recovered official ladder pages
to check every result.

Emails from 2007 and 2008 establish that complete builds were shared
between players and help date those relationships. The saved .d2s
files show exact character data. They do not, by themselves, identify
the first inventor of a mechanic or prove a particular duel outcome.

The surviving 2010 House of Legends guides are useful because they
describe the same class of strange mana, damage, regeneration, and
skill interactions I remember. They are later publications by their
own authors, not evidence that AMF invented every technique.

Finally there are my memories: the clan war, tests, friends, spying,
referees, and discoveries. I remember some much more clearly than
others. Where there's a disagreement between memory and a written
record, I say so rather than picking a version that sounds better.

For preservation, I have left private email addresses, reset tokens,
and unrelated personal details out of this public account.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
03. THE PERSON BEHIND HU_THOR AND AZREAL]AMF[
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before I was in AMF I was HU_Thor, founder of Hackers United, or HU.
We had our own channel, remembered as /j op HU1, and a reputation
to build. I wanted to challenge the people everyone already knew.

A victory against an established name could become a screenshot,
a forum post, and an argument that kept going long after the duel.
The flame forums were rough. Winning mattered, but so did making sure
people had seen the win.

AMF was the clan I especially wanted to challenge. I saw their
members as the people with the deepest builds and most closely guarded
knowledge. Public clashes, technical experiments, and the HU versus
AMF rivalry eventually led to my joining them.

I became Azreal]AMF[. That wasn't merely a tag change. I went from
trying to defeat people whose discoveries I couldn't see to discussing
those discoveries with them. We traded builds and worked out
interactions in private.

A 2006 AMF history credits Azreal with #1 in all seven 1.11 ladder
categories. That's a contemporary clan record, not a surviving full
set of official standings. I also remember leading ladders over
multiple years and using alternate accounts to investigate rivals.
I cannot independently reconstruct every match today.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
04. LADDERHALL: A COMMUNITY MADE BY ITS PLAYERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

LadderHall was player-run competition for modified characters.
Challenges had rules, ranked positions, and referees. A formal duel
could involve a host/referee and two players joining in sequence.
The join order mattered enough that competitors sometimes changed
positions across rounds.

That sounds absurd if you think of Diablo II as only a combat game.
For us it was a mechanical variable. Aura and damage behavior could
differ depending on when a character entered the game. Testing a
build in only one position could give you the wrong answer about
which build was stronger.

Rules also evolved around discoveries. An interaction was not
automatically forbidden simply because nobody had seen it before.
Something powerful could be used until it was understood, countered,
or eventually prohibited. I remember life-lock values and later
Submerge/Emerge becoming banned issues.

The forums were the other half of LadderHall. Screenshots, mockery,
clan wars, challenges, and debates built reputations. The community
was small enough that we knew the names, but its mechanics were deep
enough that we still had things to discover.

There is a gap in the surviving record: we have references and old
forum images, but not a complete verified export of all rules and
ladder standings for every patch.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
05. AMF: FOUNDING, MEMBERSHIP, AND GOVERNMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

AMF stood for Alpha Mother Fuckers. It was selective, small, and
competitive. The preserved founding account names Thanatos,
Murderin, and GodsWrath, later known as LouSahFur, among the
founders. They had connections to an earlier clan called FBI.

The written rules said to aim for no more than five active members,
recruit for both exceptional skill and activity, vote on candidates,
avoid internal drama, and have no formal ranks or leaders. Its longer
historical roster contains more than five names, which is compatible
with a limit on active members rather than total membership.

The document also discusses Dstruct, Spidey, JayOfTeRoR,
TermiNATE, and earlier generations of members. It gives AMF
substantial ladder claims across versions 1.09, 1.10, and 1.11.
Those are the clan's historical claims, not independently audited
standings.

What I remember most is the research. There was an InvisionFree
forum for calculations, experiments, and debates about mechanics.
Members shared complete characters through email, MSN, and later
Skype. This wasn't just swapping equipment lists. A whole saved
character could expose months of tests and the exact combinations
that made a build work.

AMF's trust mattered because of that secrecy. As far as I know,
no one inside the group betrayed our research before people retired.
That is my recollection, not proof that the wider dueling scene was
free of cheating or theft.

One source conflict deserves mentioning. AMF's founding prose says
it had never been in an official clan war at the time of that writing.
I remember the HU versus AMF conflict. The difference might be the
date, what counted as official, or imperfect memory. I don't know.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
06. THE HU VERSUS AMF WAR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

As HU_Thor, getting AMF's attention was part of the goal. I challenged
members, posted screenshots, and tried to find something that could
break the builds they were known for.

My friend Zak, STORMY], was an official referee. Together we
experimented with a strange interaction involving Life Tap, Smite,
and the extreme healing configurations used by high-level edited
characters. In the tests I remember, applying the curse and then
hitting certain targets could produce a delayed death that looked
connected to their own healing. Ordinary builds did not react the
same way.

A later House of Legends guide discusses overhealing deaths on huge
modified life values, so the general class of problem was real
enough to have been written about. That guide does not verify the
specific HU test or establish its exact calculation.

There is an uncomfortable part to the story. Having a friendly
referee secretly affect an official duel was not a fair match.
I was trying to win and establish a reputation, and I crossed
that boundary. It shows the darker side of the same culture that
rewarded clever research.

The rivalry eventually opened the door to joining AMF, but I
wouldn't describe those early matches as a clean scientific record.
There was ego, tactical concealment, public flaming, and sometimes
people manipulating circumstances.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
07. FRIENDSHIP, RIVALRY, APPRENTICESHIP, AND TRUST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

ADEEM / CRUCIFY]AMF[

Adeem was both a teacher and later a rival. He understood mechanics
I had not seen before. One of his builds used enormous negative
elemental resistance and absorb so that attacks expected to hurt
could instead heal him. I remember countering that by removing the
elemental auras and making it a physical fight involving Thorns.

The memory I value most is his puzzle about an extra gem. He had a
one-hit aura-killing build that required one more gem than the normal
inventory seemed able to carry. Importantly, he ALREADY KNEW the
answer. He gave me the problem because he wanted me to learn it
rather than simply tell me what to do.

While moving another player's ear around a full inventory of charms,
I realized the cursor itself could hold an item. That item could
remain there through saving, leaving, and rejoining. The cursor
was one more place to carry something.

When I reported the solution, Adeem knew I had reached the answer
he'd been guiding me to find. He gave me the character. I did not
solve a mystery he was stuck on. The point was him teaching me to
recognize possibilities outside the obvious limits of the interface.

Our rivalry later became ugly at times. I regret some of the things
I said in those arguments. His role as a mentor is more important
to me now than whatever we said in a flame thread. We reconnected
briefly years later but did not rebuild the old friendship.

JORDEN / HADES]AMF[

Jorden researched aura stacking, including rapid equipment changes
that combined Oak Sage and Prayer in unusual ways. I supported
letting him demonstrate his ideas even though he did not meet the
usual expectation that an AMF candidate already held #1.

As I remember it, he proved the builds worked, joined our circle,
and later reached #1 himself. We spent a lot of time talking about
tests and calculations. I eventually lost contact with him.

THANATOS AND THE OTHERS

Thanatos, Murderin, LouSahFur, JayOfTeRoR, and others were
important because we could argue about mechanics and hand one
another complete builds. Thanatos became a friend. Old email
records include saved-character transfers, including six
older files he sent in March 2008.

A screenshot could advertise a win, but inside the clan the
real work was sharing enough detail for someone else to check
how the result happened. That exchange made the group valuable
and also made its privacy essential.

08. THE DISCOVERIES: A RECONSTRUCTED TECHNICAL HISTORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

08A. LIFE LOCK / FUNKY MANA
[R/T] Certain exact mana values and Telekinesis/Energy Shield setups
produced the apparent near-immunity of 'life lock'. I
remembered a number close to 1,048,576, and the surviving 2010 guide
actually records 1,048,576 at Telekinesis level 30 as a special point.
Its writer describes alternating mana ranges, 'funky zero', and related
integer limits. This is independent historical corroboration of the
mathematical *tradition*, not proof of every explanation.

08B. MOSQUITO COUNTER TO LIFE LOCK
[R] Investigating why seemingly unbeatable characters occasionally died,
I noticed a special mana-altering attack from small flying
Act III monsters (remembered as Sucker/Feeder mosquitoes). Simply
assigning their attack as a modified player skill did not produce the
expected opponent-mana drain. Instead, he experimented with custom
'on kill summon' effects to spawn the particular monsters from ordinary
kills. The summons could touch enemy mana, break the magic exact value,
and expose the opponent to Smite. He worked with JayOfTeRoR on tests.
[I] The claim is not presently independently engine-verified and should
not be advertised as an established formula without reproducing it.

08C. NEGATIVE RESISTANCE AS HEALING
[R] Adeem's unusual combinations of severely negative elemental
resistance and enormous elemental absorb turned common offensive aura
approaches into healing. My answer was to remove the
auras entirely and fight with physical attacks and Thorns. The broader
lesson: your own attack may be the opponent's energy source.

08D. ONE-HIT GEM SOCKETING AND THE EAR/CURSOR REVELATION
[R] Edited gem/socket stacking could produce an enormous aura-based
one-hit effect. The final additional gem appeared impossible to carry
until the mouse cursor was recognized as an extra persistent location.
This was a collaborative discovery: Adeem's build and challenge, the
participant's independent solution to the inventory constraint. Exact
gem counts and arithmetic are not preserved here.

08E. STUN ON HIT, EXTREME SPEED, AND THORNS
[R] Modified Stun on Hit (described as NV Stun interactions) created an
abnormal offensive event or rapid attack sequence that could kill from
any join position. It also often killed the attacker because Thorns
returned catastrophic damage. Some players survived by using life lock.
My secret alternative was counterintuitive: strip almost
all physical attack damage from his items, then rely on the abnormal
attack frequency to deliver enough total damage without overwhelming
himself with return damage. More ordinary experiments used small stun
trigger values to moderate the tradeoff.
[T] The 2010 attack-speed guide separately describes skill ID 139 Stun,
10% and 90% trigger examples, and Thorns-related dangers. The precise
secret micro-damage variant remains firsthand testimony.

08F. JORDEN'S OAK SAGE / PRAYER AURA STACKING
[R] Rapidly equipping and unequipping aura equipment could produce
stacking states beyond naive item totals; combinations of Oak Sage and
Prayer achieved exceptional survival. The success reportedly carried
across join positions and became a major AMF recruitment argument.
[T] The technical guides separately discuss active aura sources,
stacking, and differences between a combined level and many sources.

08G. SUBMERGE / EMERGE
[R] A later dueler discovered that modified monster skills called
Submerge and Emerge could leave a character effectively untargetable
while its offensive aura continued damaging opponents. It was
particularly powerful on a one-hit aura build that otherwise had no
space for defense. LadderHall banned it relatively quickly. The
participant does not know of a discovered competitive counter before
that ban, though he reasonably believes a longer-lived metagame might
have found one.
[D] Hexon_iN's exported charm properties explicitly include '+63 To
Submerge' and '+63 To Emerge'.
[T] A 2010 guide's Smite/Dodge section lists Submerge, Emerge, and
related monster-state tricks; it also warns that some returned-damage
interactions such as Thorns may still affect the character. Thus 'no
one could damage you' is the historical dueling experience reported by
I, not a verified universal engine guarantee.

08H. LIFE TAP / OVERHEALING
[R] During HU's conflict with AMF, a third party casting a Life Tap-like
curse on the target reportedly induced deadly healing in highly tuned
Smite opponents with enormous Prayer. Unlike an immediate one-shot,
death appeared after some attacks and occurred much faster than normal.
[T] House of Legends later documented overhealing vulnerabilities,
though not the exact match or causal chain. Overflow is a possible
hypothesis, not a proved explanation.

08I. JOIN POSITION
[R/T] Host, second, and third join positions were competitively
meaningful. Official rematches swapped order to compare disadvantage.
The surviving guides mention aura timing and host effects. In-game
mechanics varied enough that 'same two builds' could behave differently
depending on which client entered first.

08J. THE REAL ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT
[R/T/I] Optimizers balanced incoming damage, outbound damage, Energy
Shield transfer, mana rolls, regeneration ticks, stacked auras, and the
space occupied by encoded character properties. Maximum output was not
the same as maximum winning ability. A stat might help the opponent.
A supposedly stronger build might die faster. One tiny change could
reverse immunity or healing into immediate death.

These are original paraphrased notes on historically published guides,

SOURCE A: TELEKINESIS VALUES [T]
  https://houseoflegends.forumotion.com/t59-telekinesis-values
  Posted 2010 by Sheriff; the local research notes credit DBD_TaN.
  Reported Energy Shield mana-per-damage ratios:
    TK 0     2.0000
    TK 12    1.2500
    TK 16    1.0000
    TK 24    0.5000
    TK 30    0.1250
    TK 31+   0.0625
  Approximate described ratio, before lower bound:
    2 - 0.0625 * Telekinesis level
  Why it mattered: exact TK level profoundly changed which mana
  windows could absorb damage; visually similar characters could have

SOURCE B: PHYSICAL IMMUNITY & MANA VALUES [T]
  https://houseoflegends.forumotion.com/t63-physical-immunity-mana-values
  Describes mock PI, funky PI, mana lock, Energy Shield, roll points,
  join behavior, regeneration, and Smite/Dodge.
  Key published values: 1,048,576 (TK 30 'funky' point);
  8,388,607 / 8,388,608 (boundaries in model).
  Example constructed from the guide:
    25,600 * (1 + 3,996/100) = 1,048,576.
  The guide also lists multiple special mana ranges; preserving one
  value alone is insufficient to reconstruct the full strategy.
  Critically, it documents Submerge/Emerge and notes that some reflected
  damage can still be received.

SOURCE C: LIFE VALUES & REGENERATION [T]
  https://houseoflegends.forumotion.com/t62-life-values-regeneration
  Discusses life pools in the millions and overhealing dangers.

  large values around 6–7.8 million, and Prayer stacking near level
  5,000. Its reported formulae (using aura level L) include:
    Prayer life healing = (L - 28) * 3 + 41
    Prayer mana drain   = (L - 28) * 0.376 + 6
  These formulae are quoted here for historical preservation, not
  certified game-engine calculations. The local notes credit ToXiC
  for part of the wider research.

SOURCE D: ATTACK SPEED, DAMAGES AND DEFENCE [T]
  https://houseoflegends.forumotion.com/t61-attack-speed-damages-and-defence

  return damage. Historical notes also give a Thorns relationship:
    (level - 1) * 40% + 250%
  Other discussed boundaries include 8,388,607 raw damage and a
  reported roughly 714,000 post-reduction PvP ceiling in examples.
  My zero-attack-damage response to high-speed Thorns
  is a separate memory, not directly attributed to the 2010 writer.

SOURCE E: HEXING NON CLASS SKILLS [T]
  https://houseoflegends.forumotion.com/t60-hexing-non-class-skills

11. D2SPIRATE, REFEREEING, AND THE DECLINE OF TRUST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

D2spirate was different from the ordinary competitive spying I
remember. An alternate character could help you watch an opponent's
behavior. A stolen character file could reveal their entire design.

I remember D2spirate as a program that let a game host obtain a
joining player's local .d2s save without permission. This is a
historical account of that capability; I have not reproduced its
technical operation in a present-day controlled test. An August 1,
2007 email reference to d2spirate.rar places that name in surviving
correspondence by then, but doesn't identify its original author.

Why did this matter so much? Your save contained the exact skills,
item properties, unusual values, and hidden combinations that made
you competitive. If a rival got a copy, they could inspect it,
duplicate it, or test counters in private. They could learn in
minutes what had taken someone else months to discover.

That undermined the very process that gave the ladder meaning.
To enter a legitimate challenge, you had to join somebody's
hosted game. If the host could use that access to take your
research, then merely competing exposed your strongest advantage.
Afterward you couldn't know whether an opponent had discovered
your weakness independently or just read your file.

I also remember a host-side manipulation called Gods Be Gone,
associated with changing the conditions in which a duel took
place. Unlike a new character mechanic, a secretly altered host
could make the apparent outcome of an official match unreliable.
I won't assign individual blame without stronger records.

Patches could reset builds and begin another round of research.
A breach of trust was harder to recover from. That wasn't the
only reason the scene faded: people also grew older, retired,
changed games, and lost contact. I cannot establish a single
cause or exact date when the community ended.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. WHY THIS EXPERIENCE FELT LIKE MAGIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The part I can't recreate by simply launching Diablo II is the
combination of experimentation and community.

Almost anyone could open Hero Editor and start changing a character.
That did not mean they understood overflow, aura interactions,
damage reduction, healing, or join order. There were discoveries
to make, and the people testing them could personally challenge
the best-known players. When an experiment worked, the result
could become a ranked win, a clan discussion, and a forum fight.

There was a real information game. We watched opponents, hid
accounts, tested counters, gave each other deliberately difficult
problems, and protected discoveries. I remember thinking about
builds away from the computer because a single mechanical idea
could change everything.

The gem puzzle is the memory that best explains this for me.
Adeem already knew the trick. What mattered was that he wanted
me to arrive at it myself and then trusted me with the finished
character. That was learning from somebody who understood how
to think about the game, not just receiving a stronger item.

I remember the victories, but I also remember spending time on
MSN with people who cared about an obscure calculation as much
as I did. That is the part that survived in my head after the
actual sites and conversations vanished.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. WHAT IT WOULD TAKE TO RECREATE IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

I haven't found another game that puts all these pieces together
in quite the same way. That is a personal judgment, not a claim
that no other community has ever done similar research.

A modern equivalent would need local, editable characters and
a complex enough underlying system that experimentation can keep
producing surprises. It would need opt-in multiplayer where
everyone accepts those modifications, plus visible competitions
with rules, referees, and reputations.

It would also need public places to show results and private
places to research them. Secrets would have to remain worth
protecting without hosts being able to steal the underlying
character files. The community should be small enough for
people to recognize one another, but active enough to keep
discovering new counters.

Many modern online games use server-controlled characters and
tight balance for good reasons. Those choices make the old Open
Battle.net style much harder to reproduce. Even if the software
existed, getting the same friendships, rivalries, trust, and
research culture would be the difficult part.

* Can dates and ownership of the surviving character backups be
  established beyond filenames and later recollections?

The record isn't helped by filling any of those gaps with a neat
answer. It's more useful to preserve the question, the evidence
that exists, and what I actually remember.

The two things that have stayed clearest are that discoveries
mattered more than mechanical speed, and that the people who
understood them mattered more than the ranking once the community
was gone.

FINAL THOUGHT

The deepest competitive act in this world was not clicking faster.
It was seeing an unexplored possibility in a familiar system, proving
it to another person, and changing what everyone believed was possible.

The most important discovery preserved by this keepsake may not be a
single exploit or one perfect mana value. It is the memory of a group
of people who built their own laboratory, competitive league, mythology,
and friendships inside a game, then carried that world with them long
after the servers and forums became quiet.
